    Photographing it

    Do not point a phone, camera or binoculars at the Sun without a proper solar filter over the front. Sensors burn, and so do eyes at a viewfinder.

    No filter? Shoot everything else. The crescents under a tree. The flat metallic light on buildings. Faces in the crowd. Your shadow with a hard edge on one side. These are the photos people remember, and they need no filter at all.

    The safe trick: hold your eclipse glasses flat over the phone lens and tap to focus. It works, and it costs nothing.

    Before you look

    Never look at the Sun without ISO 12312-2 eclipse glasses.

    Not sunglasses. Not a phone screen. Not smoked glass, welding glass under shade 14, or an exposed negative. A deep partial eclipse is still full-strength sunlight, and retinal damage is painless and permanent.

    Check your glasses for scratches or pinholes against a bright lamp first. If light gets through anywhere, bin them.

    No glasses? A pinhole projector works perfectly, and so does standing under a leafy tree. You lose nothing — the crescents on the ground are the best part anyway.

    The rest of the day

    This is the biggest sky day of the year

    The eclipse is the middle act. There are two others, and they're free.

    Six planets before dawn. Jupiter, Mercury, Mars, Uranus, Saturn and Neptune strung across the sky from the east round to the south-west. Jupiter, Mars, Mercury and Saturn are naked-eye; the other two need binoculars.

    The Perseids peak the same night — and the New Moon falls on 12 August, so there's no moonlight to wash them out. Under a dark sky that's often 80 or more meteors an hour. Get away from streetlights, lie back, and give your eyes twenty minutes to adjust. No equipment, no filter, no skill required.

    The next one

    2 August 2027 — six minutes of totality

    Southern Spain, North Africa and Egypt. One of the longest totalities of the century, and a completely different thing to a partial — the corona, the drop into night, the temperature crash. Worth planning a year out.
